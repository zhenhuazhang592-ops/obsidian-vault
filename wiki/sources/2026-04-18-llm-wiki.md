# Summary

This article introduces the "LLM Wiki" pattern—a methodology for building personal knowledge bases where an LLM incrementally builds and maintains a persistent wiki between you and your raw sources. Unlike traditional RAG systems that re-derive knowledge on every query, the wiki compounds over time, with cross-references, contradictions, and synthesis already in place. The LLM handles all writing, updating, and cross-referencing while humans focus on sourcing, exploration, and asking questions.

## Key Claims

1. **Persistent Wiki vs. RAG**: Traditional RAG systems retrieve relevant chunks at query time, forcing the LLM to rediscover knowledge from scratch on every question. The LLM Wiki pattern creates a persistent, compounding artifact where knowledge is compiled once and kept current, not re-derived on every query.

2. **Division of Labor**: Humans are responsible for sourcing, exploration, and asking the right questions. The LLM handles all the "grunt work"—summarizing, cross-referencing, filing, and bookkeeping that makes a knowledge base useful over time.

3. **Three-Layer Architecture**: The system consists of Raw Sources (immutable source of truth), The Wiki (LLM-generated markdown files), and The Schema (configuration document telling the LLM how to maintain the wiki).

4. **Exploration Results Compound**: When you ask a question and receive an answer—a comparison, analysis, or discovered connection—that valuable output should be filed back into the wiki as new pages, allowing exploration to compound just like ingested sources.

## Key Quotes

> "The wiki is a persistent, compounding artifact. The cross-references are already there. The contradictions have already been flagged. The synthesis already reflects everything you've read."

> "You're in charge of sourcing, exploration, and asking the right questions. The LLM does all the grunt work—the summarizing, cross-referencing, filing, and bookkeeping that makes a knowledge base actually useful over time."

> "Obsidian is the IDE; the LLM is the programmer; the wiki is the codebase."

## Operations

- **Ingest**: Process new sources by reading, discussing takeaways, writing summaries, updating index, revising entity/concept pages, and logging entries
- **Query**: Ask questions against the wiki, receive synthesized answers with citations; file valuable answers back as new wiki pages
- **Lint**: Periodically health-check for contradictions, stale claims, orphan pages, missing cross-references, and data gaps

## Index and Log

- **index.md**: Content-oriented catalog of all wiki pages with links, summaries, and metadata
- **log.md**: Chronological append-only record of all operations (ingests, queries, lint passes)
